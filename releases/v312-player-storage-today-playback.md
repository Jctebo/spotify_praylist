### John's decision (settled — not re-opened by this section)

John, Ora Pro Nobis thread, 2026-11-08 16:53 CT, chose **option (a)** from the player diagnosis, with three standing rules for the app:

1. **Storage:** the nightly run pushes the day's episode entries into the app's own D1 at publish time; the player reads D1 only; the live-RSS read path comes out. (The public feed stays the podcast's feed; its staleness is a separate matter and is not this release's problem to fix.)
2. **One-day retention:** the app store keeps one day only — each day's push replaces the prior day's episode entries and player audio (episode index rows + app-store audio for episodes and intentions). No archive in the app.
3. **Today-centered:** the app is organized around today's prayer — the Prayers tab opens on today, blocks in order; no archive or back-catalog browsing as a goal.
4. **Full playback:** Play All for the whole day and Play All per block · a continuous queue in block order · background playback (screen locked / app backgrounded) · lock-screen / media-session controls (play / pause / next / previous / seek) · autoplay-next · resume where sensible. A missing track is named plainly in place, never silently dropped from the count.

**Fold ruling:** standalone. This release stands as its own row by John's direction at registration; it does not fold into any other release, and no other release folds into it.

### Diagnosis of record — and how option (a) cures it

Verified 2026-11-08 (the v305 player report): the feed address the app reads, `https://jctebo.github.io/spotify_praylist/podcast.xml` (hard-coded as `FEED_DEFAULT` in app/worker/src/index.ts, overridable only by a harness env var), 301-redirects to `orapronobis.media/podcast.xml`, whose newest item at diagnosis time was **Nov 5, 2026** — zero current episodes at the address the app reads, so the player showed the day's prayers unavailable or absent.

Scope-pass evidence gathered 2026-11-08 (this pass, live fetch): the redirect target currently carries 2,804 items / 2,886 pubDates spanning 2026-05-02 → 2026-11-10, ≈ 2.0 MB of XML. That does not change the decision, and it sharpens the second half of the diagnosis: the app's playback depends on an external file it neither controls nor verifies, and even on a good day the read path must download and regex-parse ~2 MB at read time to find one day.

The fragile read path, verified in code (app/worker/src/index.ts, `feedEpisodes` + `playerDayPayload`):

- At request time the Worker fetches the feed, splits items with a regex, and extracts title / enclosure / pubDate with three more regexes. An item missing any one of the three is silently skipped.
- Matching is by string: the Chicago date derived from pubDate must equal the requested date, and the episode's title prefix (title minus a trailing " - Month D, YYYY") must equal either a `player_sources` title or a prayer item's name. Anything unmatched falls into an "unmapped" bucket rendered at the bottom as "From today's podcast."
- The mapping is nearly empty: production D1 holds **7 `player_sources` rows — 6 playlist (Spotify link) rows and exactly 1 rss row** ("Morning Prayer"). Every other episode track depends on the name-matching fallback against the 35 prayer items.
- A module-level cache holds the parsed result for **300,000 ms (5 minutes) — including the empty result**: a failed or stale fetch caches an empty day for five minutes.
- A fetch failure is swallowed (`catch {}`): the player renders tracks `available: false` with nothing naming the cause.

**How (a) cures both halves:** the day's episodes become rows the nightly run writes into the app's own D1 at the one moment their identity is certain — publish time, from the run's own manifest. The player then reads only its own store: no external fetch in the read path (the stale-address failure cannot occur), no parse, no prefix matching, no empty-result cache (the fragility cannot occur). What the run published is what the player shows, and a day the run did not publish reads as a named missing day, not as a silently empty one.

### Discovery findings (verified)

1. **The player today.** `GET /api/player/day?date=` builds the payload per request from three sources: `player_sources` (D1), the live feed (above), and `prayer_audio` (the Intentions segments). Blocks stand in the fixed six-block order (Morning · Midmorning · Midday · Afternoon · Evening · Night). Each block renders its matched tracks plus an Intentions card; the Intentions card is the one surface already served from the app's own store (segment-file route only, no Spotify link — Amendment 9's hard rule, preserved by this release).
2. **The client player is minimal.** One `<audio>` element in the shell (app/worker/src/ui.ts), `preload="none"`. Per-track play/pause; a per-block queue (`playQueue`) that chains on the `ended` event. There is **no Play All for the whole day**, **no Media Session API anywhere in the app** (zero hits for `mediaSession` / `MediaMetadata` / `setActionHandler` across app/), no seek or next/previous controls, no playback-position memory (no resume), and the per-block queue is built from *available tracks only* — an unavailable track is dropped from the queue silently even though its card is shown. The app also opens on the **Intentions tab** (`setTab('intentions')` at boot), and the Prayers screen carries day navigation (prev / next / date picker) — back-catalog browsing, which the today-centered rule retires.
3. **The app holds no episode data today.** Production D1 tables: the six prayer tables, `player_sources`, `prayer_audio`, the examination and confession tables. There is no episode-shaped table. `prayer_audio` holds exactly **one date — 2026-11-08, all 6 blocks** (the v305 first push). No episode index rows and no episode audio exist in the app store.
4. **The publish point.** The nightly run (job `ora-pro-nobis-nightly-run`, daily 00:00 CT, target = run date + 2) finalizes the day at its step 4, `runner/pass2_build.py --publish`: it renders, assembles, brands, loudnorms, verifies, uploads MP3 + sidecar JSON to the podcast R2 with manifest reconciliation, grafts the items onto the live feed, commits docs/podcast.xml, verifies the live feed, and flips the ledger to published — the ledger flip is last, and the step-4 timeout rule's `--reconcile` mode completes only the missing tail (ledger flip still last). The run's own record (report.json) carries, per episode: episode_id, title, kind, the built MP3 file name, duration seconds, and bytes. A real day: **2026-11-10 — 16 episodes, all built, 4,093.5 s total**. The built MP3s stand on disk in the run directory at publish time.
5. **The door pattern for the push already exists.** The app's agent door (Bearer AGENT_TOKEN, `~/workspace/.tools/prayer_door.py`) already accepts the nightly Intentions audio push: `POST /api/agent/prayer/audio` with raw MP3 bytes and X-Prayer-Date / X-Prayer-Block headers, writing R2 under `segments/<date>/<block>.mp3` and upserting `prayer_audio`. The episode push follows this exact pattern — it is an extension of a proven route shape, not a new mechanism.
6. **Audio surfaces.** Episode audio today lives only on the podcast's hosts (the feed's enclosure URLs on the podcast R2 / Pages chain) — outside the app's control, which is the point of failure. The app's own R2 bucket (`ora-pro-nobis-audio`) holds only the Intentions segments. Under this release the episode audio joins the app bucket, under the one-day rule.
7. **Constraints that stand:** John-only at Cloudflare Access, no Pam path · the Intentions card keeps its segment-file route and never carries a Spotify link · no checklist / no done state in Prayers · examination data has no agent-door route (unchanged — the push touches player data only) · the public podcast feed, RSS/Pages endpoints, R2 audio host, and Spotify IDs are untouched by this release · the GoalPost prayer module and its fallback stand untouched.

### Scope Record

- **Outcome:** the Prayers tab becomes a today player backed entirely by the app's own store: at publish time the nightly run writes the day into the app; the player reads that day — blocks in order, every track named, the full playback set working, background play with lock-screen controls verified on John's own phone.
- **In scope:**
  1. **Episode index in the app D1** (new storage; Plan settles the migration number and exact columns). One row per episode for the stored day, carrying at minimum: date · episode_id · title · block (the block the track stands under; episodes that belong to no block carry an explicit unblocked marker and render in a "From today's podcast" group after the blocks, as unmatched episodes do today) · sort within the day · duration seconds · audio R2 key · size bytes · pushed_at. Block and sort are resolved **at push time** by the pushing job, using the same prefix rule the player uses today (mapped `player_sources` titles first, then the day's prayer-item names) — the read path performs no matching at all.
  2. **Episode audio in the app store.** Each episode's MP3 is written to the app's R2 bucket under `episodes/<date>/<episode_id>.mp3`, served through the app's own audio route shape (the `prayer_audio` GET route is the model). The feed's enclosure URLs are never stored as the player's source.
  3. **One-day replacement semantics, exact:** a push for date D is a *replace-day* operation. Inside it: (i) write D's audio objects (episodes + the day's six Intentions segments, which arrive by the existing push); (ii) write D's index rows; (iii) delete every episode index row whose date is not D; (iv) delete the prior day's episode and segment audio objects and their `prayer_audio` rows for any date that is not D. After a completed push, the app store holds exactly one date across episode rows, episode audio, and segment audio. A push that fails partway leaves the prior day intact and named as the stored day — replacement completes as one verified step (read-back of row count and per-track audio presence) or not at all; the nightly report names which date the app holds.
  4. **The push point in the nightly run.** A new step in the `ora-pro-nobis-nightly-run` job sheet, executed after step 4's publish verification and **before the ledger flip** (so the reconcile tail covers it the same way: a timed-out publish reconciles the app push with the rest of the tail, ledger flip still last). Its inputs are the run's own report/manifest: the episode list (id, title, duration, bytes), the built MP3 files, and the block resolution. It also carries the v299 rule's shape: idempotent — re-running the push for the same date replaces the same day and changes nothing else. The closing report gains a named field: the app push result (date pushed · episode count · audio verified per track · the stored date after the push).
  5. **The player read path.** `playerDayPayload` reads the episode index + `prayer_audio` from D1 only. The feed fetch, the regex parse, the prefix/date matching, the 5-minute cache, and the `FEED_DEFAULT` constant come out. `player_sources` playlist rows remain only as the blocks' "Open in Spotify" links (presentation data, no longer a playback source); the single rss mapping row is retired with the read path. A request for any date other than the stored day returns the stored day named as such — there is no other day to serve.
  6. **The today-centered Prayers tab.** The Prayers tab opens on the stored day (today, under the nightly cadence), date label shown, blocks in the fixed order, tracks in push order inside each block. The prev / next / date-picker navigation comes out. The app's opening tab becomes Prayers (it currently opens on Intentions — Plan confirms this against John's "Prayers tab opens on today" wording; the four tab order itself does not change). Each block header carries its track count as "N tracks" including unavailable ones — the count is the day's count, never a filtered count.
  7. **The full playback set, each feature named:**
     - **Play All (whole day):** one control at the top of the Prayers tab; plays every available track in the day's order (block order, then push order inside the block, then the unblocked group).
     - **Play All per block:** the per-block queue control that exists today, kept and rebuilt on the stored order.
     - **Continuous queue:** one queue model for both Play All shapes; `ended` advances to the next available track automatically (autoplay-next, below); the queue is visible in place — the currently playing track is marked on its card, as today.
     - **Autoplay-next:** on track end, the next track starts without a tap, through the whole queue, including across block boundaries in a whole-day queue.
     - **Background playback:** playback continues with the screen locked and with the app backgrounded. The audio element and queue live outside the tab view (the shell-level `<audio>` element already stands outside the view; the queue state moves with it), and switching tabs never stops or resets playback.
     - **Lock-screen / media-session controls:** the Media Session API — metadata (title "Ora Pro Nobis", the track title, the podcast artwork) plus action handlers for **play, pause, next track, previous track, and seek** (seekto; seekforward/seekbackward where the surface offers them). Next/previous move within the active queue.
     - **Resume where sensible:** the player remembers (per stored day, on the device) the last track and its position; reopening the app offers the day at that track, paused, positioned — it does not autoplay sound on open. A stored position from a prior date is discarded when the stored day changes. In-queue, pausing and resuming keeps the position (native element behavior, kept).
     - **Missing-track rule:** a track whose audio is absent renders in place, in its block and order, named by its title with a plain "Not available in the app yet" line (the wording the Intentions card already uses) — it stands in the block's count, it is skipped *over* by the queue only at the moment the queue reaches it (the queue advances to the next available track and the missing card is what shows why), and it is never removed from the list or the count. A whole day not yet pushed renders the same shape: blocks in order, every expected surface named not-available — never a blank screen and never a silent empty day.
  8. **Tests:** a harness in the v305 mold (fixture day pushed through the door pattern against a scratch app instance) proving: replace-day leaves exactly one date across rows and audio · the payload for the stored day equals the pushed manifest track-for-track (titles, blocks, order, durations) · a missing-audio track renders named and counted · the queue order is the day order · the read path performs no external fetch (the harness runs with the feed address unreachable and still serves the full day).
- **Out of scope:** the public podcast feed and its freshness (separate matter, per John's decision) · Spotify playlists and the watchdog · the podcast production pipeline itself (rendering, voices, the feed graft, the ledger — all unchanged; the push consumes the publish result) · the Intentions pool, Examination, and Confession surfaces, except that segment audio joins the one-day replacement · any archive, history, or back-catalog feature · Pam access (none exists; none is added) · the GoalPost prayer module.
- **Affected users / surfaces:** John's Ora Pro Nobis app (Prayers tab + its D1/R2 store) · the nightly run's job sheet (one added step + one report field) · the app Worker's player routes and client.

### Acceptance criteria

1. After a nightly run under the new step, the app store holds exactly the target date: episode index rows for that date only, episode audio present per row, and the six Intentions segments for that date — the prior date's rows and audio are gone (verified by read-back in the run's report).
2. The Prayers tab, opened fresh, shows the stored day without any day navigation: six blocks in order, every published episode standing under its block (or the unblocked group), each with its duration; the Intentions card stands inside its block with its segment playable.
3. The player serves the full day with the feed address unreachable — no code path in the player fetches, parses, or matches an RSS feed (verified in code and by the harness).
4. Play All (whole day) plays the day's available tracks in order without further taps; Play All per block does the same within its block; autoplay-next holds across block boundaries in the whole-day queue.
5. A track with missing audio is named in place with the not-available line, stands in its block's count, and the queue passes it by advancing — the day's displayed count equals the pushed manifest's count in all cases.
6. **Background play + media-session controls verified on John's actual phone/browser surface** (the pinned criterion — see the verification mechanism below): with the phone locked, playback continues; the lock-screen controls show the track title and operate play/pause, next, previous, and seek; backgrounding the app does not stop playback.
7. Resume: closing and reopening the app on the same stored day offers the last track at its remembered position, paused; after a new day's push, no stale position is offered.
8. Nothing else changes: the four tabs and their other surfaces behave as before; the Intentions card carries no Spotify link; Access stays John-only; the public feed and the podcast pipeline are byte-untouched.

### Pinned Review criterion — verification mechanism (proposed)

Background playback and media-session behavior cannot be proven by a harness: a Worker test can prove the code registers the handlers, not that John's phone honors them. Proposal: at the **Review gate**, the main seat puts the release candidate (the test deployment shape used for v305's rehearsal, or the branch build behind the app's own Access) in front of John with a five-item check he runs on **his actual phone** (the Z Fold 8, the installed app surface): (1) start Play All, lock the phone — audio continues; (2) lock-screen pause and resume both work; (3) lock-screen next and previous change the track and the shown title; (4) lock-screen seek moves the position; (5) background the app for another app — audio continues. John's pass/fail per item is recorded as a verdict on the release (the app's verdict route, actor John) and quoted in the Review section; **Review does not pass on agent evidence alone for criterion 6** — the agent-side evidence (handler registration, metadata, queue behavior in a real-browser boot) stands beside it, not instead of it. If the installed-app surface behaves differently from the browser surface on his phone, the surface John actually uses is the one that must pass.
