- **Exposure evidence — what the WebView can see (platform documentation and field reports):** Chromium's `enumerateDevices()` + deviceId selection is a desktop-class capability. On Android Chrome/WebView, enumeration commonly returns a single audioinput — the system-routed "default" — and Bluetooth headsets are not reliably exposed as separate devices (Amazon Chime SDK issue #3135, Jan 2026: "only default device returned, Bluetooth headsets not reliably detected", on Android Chrome as on iOS); WebView device labels are a documented known issue (empty labels; the label is recoverable only from a live track — Microsoft WebView calling docs). On Android, Chromium capture follows the OS communication route: when the glasses hold the route, the "default" device IS the glasses — exactly John's field result. Consequence: a picker built on the WebView alone cannot promise the one split John wants (phone mic vs glasses) on his phone.
- **Exposure evidence — what the native shell can do (Android Developers documentation):** `AudioManager.getDevices(GET_DEVICES_INPUTS)` enumerates every physical input — built-in, Bluetooth SCO, BLE headset, USB, wired — with real product names. `AudioManager.setCommunicationDevice()` (API 31+) selects the communication device; `AudioRecord.setPreferredDevice()` routes a native recorder (Android Developers, BLE Audio overview). MODIFY_AUDIO_SETTINGS already stands granted. A method on the standing `OraDayBridgePlugin` can list the inputs and set the route at record start (and clear it at stop); the page's standing `getUserMedia({ audio: true })` then captures through the chosen route — the recording code, the IndexedDB store, and the whole v551 shape stay unchanged. OEM routing behavior varies (Samsung/Pixel), which is why the proof step below gates the build.

The control John asked for stands in the app whole — the picker is on the record card; the shell applies the pick to the system route while recording.
