# Release 0.3.8.0: Flexible Prayer Intros And Rosary Intentions

Version: 0.3.8.0
Status: SHIPPED
Last Updated: 2026-07-23
Branch: `enhancement/flexible-prayer-intros-rosary-intentions`

## Scope

### Problem
- Generated Morning Prayer and Rosary introductions sound scripted because prompts and validators require exact sentence counts, positions, and opening phrases.
- Auxilium Christianorum, Marian Antiphon, and novena transitions are fixed templates, so the same constructions recur across episodes.
- Rosary reflections currently treat solemnities, feasts, memorials, and optional memorials as one priority class.
- The shared daily theme can overwrite the focus selected by the Rosary resolver, obscuring the intended liturgical hierarchy.
- The Rosary has an opening focus and decade reflections, but it does not have a distinct overall intention or a distinct intention before each decade.

### Audience
- Primary user: a listener praying the daily Ora Pro Nobis prayer lineup, especially the Daily Rosary.
- Secondary stakeholder: the publisher reviewing generated devotional prose for Catholic accuracy, natural spoken rhythm, and consistency across a day.

### Current Status Quo
- Morning Prayer uses an LLM but requires formulaic sentence openings such as “In today’s Gospel” and “Today’s focus is.”
- The Rosary intro uses an LLM at low variation but requires its first two sentences to follow a fixed structure.
- Each Rosary decade reflection must use exactly four sentences and repeat the mystery title and fruit.
- Prayer-transition blocks and novena welcome fragments are deterministic prose templates rather than generated devotional writing.
- Rosary focus selection is winner-take-all, groups memorials with solemnities and feasts, and may be relabeled by shared-theme data after selection.

### Goal
- Make generated devotional prose sound natural and prayerful while remaining grounded in supplied liturgical facts.
- Add one overall Rosary intention near the beginning and one short, distinct intention before each of the five decades.
- Give the LLM an explicit ordered priority payload and require the highest available priority to shape the opening intention, all five decade intentions, and all five reflections.
- Use these seasonal priority orders:
  - Ordinary Time: solemnity/feast, Gospel, memorial, Ordinary Time reflection.
  - Advent, Christmas, Lent, Easter, and other non-Ordinary seasons: solemnity/feast, seasonal reflection, Gospel, memorial.
- Let lower-priority sources support the dominant priority naturally without displacing it.
- Preserve each mystery and its fruit as the decade-specific lens through which the dominant daily priority is applied.
- Replace phrase-level validation with semantic validation of required facts, structure, safety, and completeness.
- Success criteria:
  - The opening intention and all five decades share one coherent dominant daily focus.
  - Every decade contains exactly one intention and one reflection tied to its mystery and fruit.
  - Memorials no longer receive solemnity/feast priority.
  - Non-Ordinary seasons outrank the Gospel when no solemnity or feast is present.
  - Generated intros are not required to reuse fixed sentence openings.
  - Missing model or source data still yields complete, valid spoken prayer through deterministic fallbacks.
  - Canonical prayer text remains byte-for-byte unchanged.

### Constraints
- Do not invent saint biographies, current events, quotations, doctrines, Gospel details, or liturgical facts.
- The LLM may only use the supplied calendar, season, Gospel, mystery, fruit, and approved devotional context as factual sources.
- Human needs such as families, conversion, peace, suffering, the Church, and the dead may be selected devotionally without asserting unsupplied current events.
- Output must remain concise, reverent, Catholic, suitable for TTS, and free of markdown or production commentary.
- The five decades must remain coherent around one dominant priority rather than distributing different source priorities across decades.
- Generated intentions must be structurally separate fragments so spoken pacing, caching, sidecars, readable text, and resume markers remain auditable.
- Deterministic fallback content must follow the same seasonal priority rules and complete the entire Rosary when generation fails.

### Narrow Wedge
- First implement the complete Daily Rosary experience:
  - Resolve a typed, ordered seasonal priority context once per target date.
  - Select one dominant priority without allowing shared-theme display text to overwrite it.
  - Generate one overall intention.
  - Generate five paired decade intentions and reflections using the same dominant priority plus each mystery and fruit.
  - Relax exact-phrase and exact-sentence validators in favor of semantic and structural validation.
  - Add equivalent deterministic fallbacks and full unit/integration coverage.
- Reuse the resulting flexible devotional-prose policy for Morning Prayer and other prayer transitions only after the Rosary contract proves the approach.

### Non-Goals
- Changing the weekday Rosary mystery schedule or canonical Rosary prayers.
- Assigning different priority sources to different decades.
- Generating or summarizing live news as prayer intentions.
- Rewriting traditional novena bodies or other source-owned devotional texts.
- Removing deterministic fallback behavior.
- Making the LLM responsible for calculating liturgical rank or season.
- Broadly rewriting every generated prayer surface in the first implementation wedge.

### Risks And Unknowns
- “Semantic” validators must be strong enough to catch omitted priorities, missing intentions, invented facts, or malformed output without forcing formulaic prose again.
- A single generation request may produce more coherent prose, while separate requests may offer better retry and fragment-cache isolation; research should compare these boundaries.
- The model may over-repeat the dominant focus across six intentions and five reflections unless prompts explicitly require varied applications and language.
- Generated human-need categories could become repetitive or unbalanced over time; a history-aware diversity mechanism may eventually be useful but is not required for the first wedge.
- Existing episode fragment hashes and caches will change when intention fragments are added.
- The current Rosary title uses shared focus fields; the listener-facing title must remain aligned with the newly selected dominant priority.

### Recommended Next Step
- Run `/research-astack` to trace every Rosary context, prompt, validator, fallback, fragment, title, sidecar, and test dependency.
- Research should recommend:
  - the typed priority payload and rank classification boundary;
  - whether generation should return one structured response or independently retryable components;
  - semantic validation rules that preserve freedom without sacrificing factual grounding;
  - the exact fragment order for the overall intention and each decade intention;
  - the smallest reusable devotional-prose interface that can later improve Morning Prayer and fixed prayer transitions.

## Research

### Scope Baseline
- Improve the Daily Rosary first: replace formulaic intro/reflection prose with one coherent generated devotional package containing a natural introduction, one overall intention, and five mystery-specific intention/reflection pairs.
- Resolve one dominant priority for the whole Rosary:
  - Ordinary Time: solemnity/feast, Gospel, memorial, Ordinary Time reflection.
  - Other seasons: solemnity/feast, seasonal reflection, Gospel, memorial.
- Keep lower priorities available as supporting context, but do not let them replace or dilute the dominant source.
- Preserve all canonical Rosary prayers and the weekday mystery schedule.

### Current System Map
- Publish entrypoints load `config/publish/contracts/*.json` and call `build_text_jobs` or `build_audio_jobs`.
- A `DailyThemeRuntimeCache` resolves the shared liturgical payload once per date/calendar/locale and copies it into each entry runtime.
- The Rosary runtime then independently resolves mystery text, Gospel context, Romcal rows, season, and `RosaryDayContext`.
- Current Rosary priority is:
  - any solemnity, feast, memorial, or optional memorial -> `feast`;
  - otherwise Gospel;
  - otherwise season;
  - otherwise mystery fruits.
- After selecting that source, shared-theme values overwrite `focus_title` and `focus_prompt_label` while leaving `focus_source` unchanged. A job can therefore report one source while prompting and titling with another focus.
- The intro is a separate plain-text model call at temperature `0.3`. It requires 3-4 sentences, a season reference in sentence one, and the exact opening “For today’s rosary, we will focus on” in sentence two.
- Reflections are generated in a second call at temperature `0.4`. The preferred path uses `responses.parse` with Pydantic, then falls back to five plain-text lines.
- Reflection validation checks only count and a 650-character maximum. Prompt-only rules require four sentences and mystery/fruit repetition; those rules are not programmatically validated.
- The reflection branch is selected from `focus_source`: feast-specific, Gospel, or season/generic. It cannot express the requested season-dependent ordered priority list.
- Audio currently renders:
  - one Rosary intro fragment;
  - fixed opening-prayer fragments;
  - for each decade, one combined heading/fruit/reflection fragment followed by fixed Our Father, ten Hail Marys, Glory Be, and Fatima Prayer fragments;
  - fixed closing-prayer fragments.
- Readable text uses the same generated reflection set but groups all five decades into one top-level text section.
- Audio sidecars already persist every fragment’s text and stable key. Rosary summary metadata currently records only generation source, fallback reason, count, focus source, focus title, and prompt label.
- `audio_manifest_hash` includes the ordered fragment set; new intention fragments will intentionally rebuild the episode while unchanged canonical-prayer fragments retain their individual cache hits.

### Reuse Opportunities
- Keep `DailyThemeRuntimeCache` as the cross-prayer calendar/Gospel fetch cache and use its payload as supporting input; do not let shared display prose overwrite the Rosary’s selected dominant priority.
- Reuse `infer_celebration_rank`, but normalize ranks into explicit Rosary buckets:
  - major celebration: solemnity, feast, and the Easter Octave pseudo-rank where appropriate;
  - memorial: memorial and optional memorial;
  - weekday/other.
- Replace `RosaryReflectionSet` with a richer immutable devotional package containing:
  - day/priority context;
  - introduction;
  - overall intention;
  - exactly five ordered decade records with mystery, fruit, intention, and reflection;
  - generation source and fallback reason.
- Extend the existing Pydantic structured-output pattern to one response containing the introduction, overall intention, dominant-priority key, and five numbered intention/reflection pairs. One call best supports coherence and anti-repetition across the whole Rosary.
- Retain the current structured-first/provider-compatible fallback pattern:
  - `responses.parse` with Pydantic when supported;
  - plain JSON generation plus local Pydantic parsing for compatible alternate base URLs;
  - one complete deterministic devotional package when generation or semantic validation fails.
- Keep semantic checks separate from sentence templates:
  - exact five numbered decades in order;
  - non-empty bounded intro, overall intention, decade intentions, and reflections;
  - returned dominant-priority key must match the resolver;
  - each intention/reflection pair must include an approved dominant-focus anchor and its mystery title or fruit somewhere in the pair;
  - reject markdown, prompt echoes, unsupported labels, and unsupplied proper-name/citation claims where deterministically detectable;
  - do not require an exact sentence count, sentence position, or opening phrase.
- Reuse `_fragment_payload`, stable `fragment_key` paths, resume-marker generation, and existing fixed-prayer fragments.
- Recommended audio order:
  - introduction;
  - overall intention;
  - fixed opening prayers;
  - for each decade: mystery announcement, decade intention, decade reflection, then the canonical decade prayers;
  - fixed closing prayers.
- Use separate intention and reflection fragments even if both come from one model response. This preserves TTS pacing, auditability, resume markers, and fine-grained cache behavior.
- Keep Spotify matching unchanged because it is already insulated from variable focus titles.

### External References
- OpenAI Structured Outputs supports Python schemas defined with Pydantic and is appropriate for the nested devotional package: `https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs`.
- The OpenAI guidance requires callers to handle refusals and missing parsed output explicitly; the current code only checks `output_parsed` before falling back.
- Pydantic field/model validators can enforce cross-field integrity after schema parsing: `https://pydantic.dev/docs/validation/latest/concepts/validators/`.
- Repository dependencies are currently unpinned for `openai` and `pydantic`. The researched environment has OpenAI Python `2.24.0` and Pydantic `2.13.0b2`; implementation should avoid beta-only behavior or pin tested versions deliberately.

### Constraints
- `gpt-4.1-mini` is the contract default, but `OAI_MODEL` and `OAI_API_BASE_URL` can override it; the fallback path must remain compatible with providers that do not implement `responses.parse`.
- The model must not calculate liturgical season or rank. Python must resolve and serialize the ordered priority list before generation.
- Shared-theme payload version `daily-theme-v1` is consumed by other prayers and novena sidecars. Changing its global semantics would expand the release beyond the Rosary wedge.
- Current seasonal theme derivation is intentionally small:
  - Advent -> hopeful waiting;
  - Christmas -> gratitude;
  - Lent -> repentance;
  - Holy Week -> sacrificial love;
  - Easter -> resurrection hope;
  - otherwise -> trust.
- Raw Gospel text is available to the Rosary generator; the shared payload otherwise carries only a heuristic Gospel theme and citation.
- Text and audio job builders have separate runtime caches. A single job build reuses generated content internally, but independently running text and audio builds may generate different prose.
- Canonical prayer fragments must keep their text and effective TTS hash inputs unchanged; adding a top-level intention block may shift their path-derived fragment keys without invalidating the content-addressed audio cache.
- Sidecar schema additions must remain JSON-safe and archive-compatible.

### Risks
- The largest correctness risk is semantic grounding: JSON schema guarantees shape, not that prose truly emphasizes the selected priority or avoids invented facts.
- The existing shared-theme overwrite can create mismatched title, metadata, prompt, and fallback focus unless it is removed from the Rosary authority path.
- Easter Octave’s pseudo-rank can fall through as non-major unless rank normalization explicitly handles it.
- A single structured generation is intentionally atomic. One invalid decade should trigger a retry or one coherent full fallback, not a mixture of unrelated generated and fallback prose.
- Six intentions and five reflections can repeat vocabulary even with more flexible prompts. The prompt must assign distinct human applications to each decade while maintaining one dominant theme.
- Adding separate decade intention/reflection fragments increases resume-marker count and changes episode/content hashes.
- Existing tests encode the old rule that optional memorials and memorials are feast priority; these assertions must be replaced rather than merely extended.
- The unpinned/beta dependency state can make structured parsing behavior vary across CI and local runs.

### Assumptions To Validate
- “Memorials” includes both obligatory memorials and optional memorials at the same lower priority.
- Easter Octave weekdays should use the non-Ordinary seasonal priority path and remain Easter-centered rather than being promoted to a solemnity/feast theme solely because of the internal pseudo-rank.
- “Ordinary Time reflection” should be a broad prompt source grounded in Christian discipleship and the mystery fruit, not the current single fallback theme of `trust`.
- Multiple simultaneous solemnity/feast rows should be combined into one major-celebration priority item; any memorial rows remain supporting context.
- The overall intention should be spoken after the introduction and before the fixed opening prayers.

### Unknowns
- Whether generated Rosary prose must be identical when text and audio jobs are run separately. Achieving that requires a persisted generation artifact or deterministic seed/cache beyond the current in-memory runtime.
- Whether the first implementation should pin `openai` and `pydantic` versions or only add compatibility tests around the supported SDK surface.
- The final prose length bounds and TTS pause duration between intention and reflection need to be selected during planning.

### Recommended Direction
- Keep the shared daily context unchanged for other prayer families and introduce a Rosary-owned typed priority resolver.
- Make that resolver authoritative for Rosary title, prompt, generated package, fallbacks, and audit metadata. Shared-theme data becomes supporting context only.
- Resolve availability first, then apply the season-specific ordering in Python and pass the complete ordered list plus one explicit dominant key to the model.
- Generate one structured `RosaryDevotionalSet` per job build, validate it structurally and semantically, and cache it in the existing entry runtime context.
- On structured-output incompatibility, parse plain JSON into the same Pydantic model. On generation or semantic failure, replace the entire package with a deterministic package following the same priority rules.
- Expand the contract/runtime assembly with distinct overall-intention, decade-intention, and decade-reflection fragments while preserving every canonical prayer fragment.
- Extend sidecar metadata with priority order, dominant source/key, intention count, reflection count, generation source, and fallback reason; prose remains auditable through the existing fragment list.
- Plan focused tests for Ordinary Time and every non-Ordinary season, major celebrations, memorial/optional memorial behavior, Easter Octave, missing Gospel/Romcal/model data, structured/plain/deterministic generation paths, title alignment, fragment order, cache reuse, sidecars, text jobs, and Spotify compatibility.
- Baseline verification: `python -m unittest tests.test_publish_rosary_reflections tests.test_publish_contracts` passed 34 tests. A broader combined command exceeded 120 seconds and is not recorded as a pass.

## Plan

### Summary
- Build one authoritative, season-aware Rosary priority context and one generated devotional package per job build.
- The devotional package will contain a flexible introduction, one overall intention, and five ordered decade records containing a distinct human-need category, intention, and reflection.
- One dominant priority will shape every generated part; lower priorities remain supporting context.
- Use structured output plus semantic validation, one provider-compatible JSON retry, then an atomic deterministic fallback for the complete package.
- Integrate intentions as separate text/audio fragments while leaving every canonical prayer text and Spotify lookup rule unchanged.

### What Already Exists
- `jobs/publish/rosary_reflections.py` already parses five mysteries/fruits, resolves Romcal and Gospel context, calls OpenAI, uses Pydantic structured output, validates output, and builds fallbacks.
- `jobs/publish/daily_theme_runtime.py` already caches one shared daily context per date/calendar/locale.
- `jobs/publish/contracts.py` already caches Rosary generated content inside an entry runtime and renders one shared result into metadata, text, audio fragments, and resume markers.
- `jobs/publish/fragments.py` already provides stable fragment hashing and explicit `pause` control fragments.
- `jobs/publish/audio.py` already writes JSON-safe Rosary metadata and the complete ordered fragment list into the episode sidecar.
- `config/spotify/contracts/daily-rosary.json` already matches only `Daily Rosary` plus the publish date.

### Architecture
- Keep the implementation in `jobs/publish/rosary_reflections.py`; do not introduce a second overlapping Rosary generation module.
- Add immutable runtime types:
  - `RosaryPriority`: `key`, `source`, `title`, `prompt_context`, `anchors`, and audit metadata.
  - Expanded `RosaryDayContext`: mysteries, season mode, ordered available priorities, dominant priority, celebrations, Gospel data, and shared supporting context.
  - `RosaryDecadeDevotional`: mystery, human-need category, intention, and reflection.
  - `RosaryDevotionalSet`: introduction, overall intention, five decade records, day context, generation source, and fallback reason.
- Add Pydantic response types:
  - `_StructuredRosaryDecade`: `number`, `human_need_category`, `intention`, `reflection`.
  - `_StructuredRosaryDevotionalResponse`: `dominant_priority_key`, `introduction`, `overall_intention`, and exactly five decades.
- Approved human-need categories:
  - `families`
  - `church`
  - `conversion`
  - `peace`
  - `suffering`
  - `poor_and_vulnerable`
  - `dead`
  - `vocations`
  - `leaders`
  - `personal_needs`
- Require five distinct categories per generated Rosary. This gives each decade a different application without distributing the dominant liturgical priority.
- Resolve priorities in Python:
  - Ordinary Time: major celebration -> Gospel -> memorial -> Ordinary Time.
  - Non-Ordinary season: major celebration -> season -> Gospel -> memorial.
  - Unknown season/data failure: major celebration -> Gospel -> memorial -> mystery fruits.
- Rank handling:
  - Major celebration: `solemnity`, `feast`.
  - Memorial: `memorial`, `optional_memorial`.
  - `solemnity-easter octave` remains an Easter-season source, not a major-celebration override.
  - Combine multiple simultaneous solemnity/feast names into one dominant major-celebration item.
- Season handling:
  - Normalize Ordinary Time separately.
  - Treat Advent, Christmas, Lent, Holy Week, and Easter as non-Ordinary.
  - Use the shared daily payload as a season/rank fallback if the Rosary’s direct Romcal lookup fails.
  - Ordinary Time reflection is broad discipleship grounded in the selected mystery and fruit; it is not hard-coded to `trust`.
- Title handling:
  - `rosary_focus_title` comes only from the authoritative dominant priority.
  - Major celebration -> joined celebration names.
  - Gospel -> `Today's Gospel`.
  - Memorial -> joined memorial names.
  - Season -> normalized season label.
  - Ordinary/final fallback -> `Ordinary Time` or `Mystery Fruits`.
- Generation handling:
  - One `responses.parse` call at configurable temperature `0.65`.
  - If structured parsing, refusal handling, or semantic validation fails, make one `responses.create` plain-JSON retry and parse it locally with the same Pydantic model.
  - If the retry fails, create one complete deterministic `RosaryDevotionalSet`; never mix generated and fallback decades.
- Semantic validation:
  - Introduction: 120-700 characters.
  - Overall intention: 60-400 characters.
  - Each decade intention: 40-280 characters.
  - Each reflection: 120-650 characters.
  - Exactly five records numbered 1-5 and exactly five distinct approved human-need categories.
  - Returned dominant key must equal the Python-selected dominant key.
  - The introduction plus overall intention and every decade intention/reflection pair must contain at least one approved dominant-priority anchor.
  - Every decade pair must contain either its mystery title or its fruit.
  - Reject markdown, prompt/instruction echoes, and detected Scripture citations other than the supplied Gospel citation.
  - Do not require exact sentence counts, sentence positions, or stock opening phrases.
- Prompt handling:
  - Supply the complete ordered priority list, mark the dominant item explicitly, and state that supporting items cannot displace it.
  - Supply only factual calendar/Gospel/mystery/fruit context.
  - Require distinct applications and varied language across the five decades.
  - Prohibit live-news claims, invented biographies, invented quotations, and facts absent from the supplied context.
- Runtime flow:

```text
target date + weekday mystery file
              |
              v
   shared day cache + Rosary Romcal/Gospel facts
              |
              v
     ordered Rosary priorities (Python)
              |
              v
  one structured devotional generation attempt
              |
       valid? +---- no ----> JSON retry
         |                       |
        yes                 valid? +-- no --> full deterministic set
         |                       |
         +-----------+-----------+
                     v
       cached RosaryDevotionalSet
          |          |          |
          v          v          v
       metadata    text     audio fragments/sidecar
```

### UX And States
- No interactive UI or responsive/accessibility work is in scope; this is a batch-generated text/audio experience.
- Listener order:
  - Natural introduction to the day and dominant focus.
  - Overall Rosary intention.
  - Existing opening prayers.
  - For each mystery: mystery/fruit announcement, distinct intention, short pause, reflection, then canonical decade prayers.
  - Existing closing prayers.
- Success state:
  - Title, opening intention, five intentions, five reflections, metadata, and sidecar all identify the same dominant priority.
  - Each decade applies that focus to a different approved human need.
- Provider-error state:
  - The listener receives a complete deterministic Rosary in the same structure; no error prose is spoken.
  - Sidecar metadata records fallback source and reason.
- Missing-calendar/readings state:
  - Use shared cached context when available.
  - Otherwise apply the explicit unknown-data fallback order and ultimately mystery fruits.
- Text and audio builds run separately today and may generate different valid prose. Persisted cross-run identity is intentionally deferred; each individual episode remains internally coherent and auditable.

### Failure Modes
- Romcal unavailable:
  - Detect from exception/fallback reason.
  - Recover season/rank from shared daily context; otherwise use Gospel, memorial, or mystery-fruit fallback order.
- Gospel unavailable:
  - Omit Gospel as an available priority and continue with the correct seasonal hierarchy.
- Easter Octave pseudo-rank:
  - Normalize to Easter seasonal focus instead of major-feast focus.
- Structured output unsupported or refused:
  - Attempt one plain-JSON response and local Pydantic validation.
- Generated package has wrong count/order/category/key:
  - Reject the entire package, retry once, then use the complete deterministic set.
- Generated prose omits dominant/mystery grounding or adds another Scripture citation:
  - Reject semantically and follow the same retry/fallback path.
- One generated decade fails:
  - Do not mix sources; replace the whole package to preserve coherence.
- Missing OpenAI key/model/provider outage:
  - Skip successful generation and publish the deterministic set.
- Fragment assembly fails:
  - Existing publish failure behavior remains; do not emit a partial episode.
- Title/metadata drift:
  - Integration tests require all surfaces to use the same dominant priority object.
- Separate text/audio runs differ:
  - Accepted for this release and explicitly documented; each run is internally coherent.
