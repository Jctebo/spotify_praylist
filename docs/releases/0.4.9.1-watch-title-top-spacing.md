# Release 0.4.9.1 - watch-title-top-spacing

- Status: SHIPPED
- Created / updated: 2026-09-28
- Base: 0.4.9.0 / `4bc9f0d`
- Branch: `enhancement/watch-title-top-spacing`
- Source: user feedback from observing the Saint Wenceslaus image on a Galaxy Watch Classic.

## Scope

### Problem
- Watch labels sat low and the dark top fade extended farther than needed, leaving excess dark space above the devotional scene.

### Audience
- The operator using devotional watch wallpapers on a recent Samsung Galaxy Watch Classic.

### Current Status Quo
- Watch title text began at 16% of image height, with a top fade extending to 40%.
- Existing generated wallpapers were left untouched as requested.

### Goal
- Move future watch title labels slightly upward and shorten the top fade while preserving legibility and circular safe-area fitting.
- Phone rendering and existing image files remain unchanged.

### Constraints
- Watch-only renderer adjustment; no generation, rotation, or modification of existing OneDrive images.
- Preserve the existing daily ten-day generation and OneDrive rotation behavior.

### Narrow Wedge
- Move the watch title cluster up four percentage points and shorten the top fade by five percentage points.

### Non-Goals
- Phone layout changes, prompt/content changes, running the generation pipeline, or modifying existing assets.

### Risks And Unknowns
- Moving labels upward brings them closer to the top edge of the circular display; the renderer continues fitting them against its circular safe area.

### Recommended Next Step
- Daily scheduled runs will apply the new layout only when future watch images are generated.

## Research

- The watch renderer uses separate fixed title-top and fade-end fractions from the phone layout.
- `_draw_centered_label` fits labels against the circular chord, and local QA reads a dedicated upper watch text region.
- Existing tests cover dimensions, long labels, OCR, and safe-circle width.

## Plan

- Changed only the watch layout fractions in `overlay_wallpaper_text`; phone dimensions and rendering remained untouched.
- Validated the devotional wallpaper tests and reviewed the diff before preparing the release.
- No image generation or rotation workflow was run.
